04 - The operating layer

Recruiting is
about a fifth
of the problem.

Finding the person is the part everyone sells. The reason India hires quietly fail is everything that comes after: no desk, no system, no visibility, no one holding quality, no plan when somebody leaves. away.center runs that layer.

05 - The workspace

Nobody does
their best work
on a bed.

This is the part of the model that's hardest to copy and easiest to underrate. Your team works from an away centre - a designed room with other people in it, meeting infrastructure, and a door between work and home. It's infrastructure, not a perk.

Somewhere to actually go

A commute that ends at a desk changes how a working day is structured. Deep work has a room; home stays home.

People on the floor

Your operator sits among other people doing serious work. Isolation is the most common reason a remote India hire quietly disengages.

Rooms when it matters

Meeting and conference rooms with proper AV for your calls, reviews and onboarding sessions — booked, not improvised.

A reason to stay

away centers run events, wellness sessions and a café. A workplace people like being in is a retention mechanism, not decoration.

away centers operate in Bangalore, Vizag and Kolkata. away Pods currently deploys from Bangalore; Kolkata and Vizag follow as workflows mature.

then it runs.

No client goes live without a role card, a WorkOS setup and a reporting cadence. That's the whole discipline - most of what goes wrong with distributed teams is a step that got skipped here.

    11 - Nano GCC

    Your first India office
    doesn't have to start
    as an office project.

    A Nano GCC is a small India operating cell - usually starting at three people and growing to five, ten, fifteen. The same intent as a global capability centre, without the entity, the lease, the HR build and the eighteen-month runway before anyone does useful work.

    It isn't a separate product. It's where the ladder ends up when a company keeps adding roles and workflows and eventually wants them to behave like one team.
